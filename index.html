What to Do If You Smell Gas

Gas leaks are life-threatening emergencies. Knowing what to do in the first 60 seconds could save your family.

Urgent Safety
If you smell gas:
1. Do NOT turn any lights or switches on or off
2. Open all doors and windows immediately
3. Turn off the gas at the meter (the handle should be at a right angle to the pipe)
4. Get everyone out of the house
5. Call the National Gas Emergency line: 0800 111 999 (free, 24/7)
6. Do NOT go back inside until an engineer says it is safe

Never use your phone, light matches, or use any electrical devices near the leak. Even a doorbell spark can ignite gas.
⚠️

Carbon Monoxide: The Silent Killer

CO is odourless and invisible. Over 40 people die from CO poisoning in the UK each year. Here is how to protect yourself.

Urgent Safety
Warning signs of CO:
• Headaches, dizziness, nausea, breathlessness
• Yellow or orange flames instead of blue on your boiler or hob
• Dark staining around or on gas appliances
• Pilot lights that frequently blow out

Protect yourself:
• Fit an audible CO alarm in every room with a gas appliance (costs as little as £15)
• Get appliances serviced annually by a Gas Safe registered engineer
• Never block air vents or flues
• If the alarm sounds: open windows, get out, call 0800 111 999
📋

Understanding Your CP12 Certificate

A CP12 is your Gas Safety Certificate. Landlords must have one annually. Here is what it means and what to check.

Guide
What a CP12 includes:
• Every gas appliance in the property inspected
• Flue and ventilation checks
• Gas pressure readings (operating and standing)
• Safety device checks
• Pass or fail for each appliance

What to check:
• The engineer's Gas Safe number (verify at gassaferegister.co.uk)
• All appliances are listed and marked as safe
• The date — it expires after 12 months
• Any "at risk" or "immediately dangerous" flags

Landlords: you must give a copy to every tenant within 28 days of the check, or before they move in.

When Does Your Boiler Need Replacing?

Boilers last 10-15 years on average. Knowing the signs saves you from breakdowns in the middle of winter.

Homeowner Tip
Signs your boiler needs replacing:
• It is over 12-15 years old
• Frequent breakdowns or repair bills over £300/year
• Strange banging, whistling, or gurgling noises
• Radiators take a long time to heat up
• Energy bills increasing without explanation
• The flame is yellow or orange (should be blue)
• It is rated below A for energy efficiency

Modern boilers are 90-94% efficient vs 60-70% for older models. Replacing a 15-year-old boiler could save you £200-£400 per year on gas bills. Through HomeLinq, you can get up to 3 quotes from local Gas Safe engineers for free.

Ask the Community

Real questions from homeowners, answered by verified Gas Safe engineers.

QMy boiler keeps losing pressure every few days. Is this dangerous?
Not immediately dangerous, but it does need attention. A slow pressure drop usually means a leak somewhere in the system — often a radiator valve or the pressure relief valve dripping outside. Top it up using the filling loop, but if it drops below 1 bar again within a week, call an engineer. Don't ignore it long-term as it can damage the boiler pump.
Answered by 🔧 Dan K. • Gas Safe Verified • 47 people found this helpful
QHow often should I get my gas fire serviced? We only use it a few times a year.
Every 12 months, regardless of how often you use it. Gas fires can develop cracks in the combustion chamber even when not in use, and an unserviced open-flued fire is one of the most common sources of carbon monoxide in UK homes. A service takes about 30 minutes and typically costs around £60-80.
Answered by 🔧 Spencer G. • Gas Safe Verified • 32 people found this helpful
QMy landlord hasn't given me a CP12 certificate. What are my rights?
Your landlord is legally required to provide a valid gas safety certificate (CP12) within 28 days of the annual check, or before you move in. If they haven't, contact them in writing first. If they still don't comply, you can report them to the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) or your local council. It is a criminal offence for a landlord not to have a valid CP12.
Answered by 🔧 James H. • Gas Safe Verified • 91 people found this helpful
